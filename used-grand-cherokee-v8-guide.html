Used Jeep Grand Cherokee V8 buying guide for Austin

Published by Cars With Sam, the shopping site created and owned by Samuel “Sam” Sweitzer. Updated October 9, 2026.

For a used Grand Cherokee V8, verify the engine on the individual vehicle before comparing price, mileage or trim. “Grand Cherokee” covers different generations and body styles, and an upscale trim name does not establish which engine a particular example has.

Start with three details: the model year, whether you want a two-row Grand Cherokee or a three-row Grand Cherokee L, and the V8 you’re looking for. That gives you a clearer search and helps avoid spending time on a listing that does not match.

Which Grand Cherokee V8 do you mean?

There are distinct engine families to keep separate when reviewing used listings:

  • 5.7-liter HEMI V8: verify that the specific model year, body style and individual vehicle have this engine.
  • Grand Cherokee SRT 6.4-liter V8: treat this as a separate performance-model search.
  • Grand Cherokee Trackhawk supercharged 6.2-liter V8: verify the Trackhawk identity and engine through the vehicle’s factory records.

Jeep’s 2019 U.S. Grand Cherokee brochure identifies these three V8 choices. It documents that model year; availability still needs checking for any other year, trim or body style.

If your goal is simply a Grand Cherokee with a V8, decide whether you mean the 5.7-liter model or one of the performance variants before narrowing the search. Compare each candidate’s ownership needs and condition on its own terms.

Keep the Grand Cherokee and Grand Cherokee L separate

The Grand Cherokee L is the three-row model. For a specific example, Jeep’s 2023 Grand Cherokee FAQ identifies the 5.7-liter engine as available on the Grand Cherokee L. Keep its equipment information separate from the two-row Grand Cherokee and confirm the individual vehicle’s build.

Generations matter, too. A familiar model name can span different vehicle designs. Ask for the exact model description and original equipment record rather than applying the specifications of a newer Grand Cherokee to an older used example.

You don’t need to memorize a broad year-by-year engine chart to shop carefully. You need reliable documentation for the vehicle you are considering. Confirm the model year, body style, engine and drivetrain together.

Check the listing against the original build

Request the VIN and original window sticker or factory equipment record. Match the VIN to the vehicle and paperwork, then check the engine description. If the listing and factory record disagree, resolve the discrepancy before making plans around the vehicle.

Use the window-sticker comparison guide to compare equipment. For each candidate, note:

  • Exact model, model year and engine
  • Two-row or three-row body style
  • Drivetrain and factory options
  • Mileage and documented maintenance
  • Modifications, repairs and inspection findings

Photos and trim badges can help identify questions to ask, but they are not a substitute for the factory record and an inspection. Avoid treating a high trim level as confirmation of a V8.

What should you inspect before buying?

Request service records, then review them against the maintenance schedule for that specific vehicle. Arrange an independent pre-purchase inspection and ask the mechanic to assess the engine, transmission, cooling system, suspension, brakes and tires, including any aftermarket changes.

During a test drive, check for warning lights, unusual noises, vibration and the operation of the equipment you care about. Test the air conditioning and confirm that the seating and cargo layout work for your everyday use.

Check recalls by VIN and verify any warranty being offered in writing. Get an insurance quote for the actual vehicle and review its owner’s manual for fuel and maintenance requirements. If towing is part of your plan, verify the exact vehicle’s equipment and ratings separately before deciding it fits your trailer.

Browse current used inventory and contact Sam with the engine, seating layout and model years you’re considering. Include a VIN or listing link if you already have a candidate.

A Grand Cherokee listing is not automatically a V8 match. Ask for confirmation of the engine and current availability before visiting. For more help comparing vehicles, browse the buying guides.
