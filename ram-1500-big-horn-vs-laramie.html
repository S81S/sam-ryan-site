Why can two Big Horn trucks have different screens?

An equipment group can replace the standard radio and add other features. Our documented 2026 Lone Star example compares R12316 with an 8.4-inch screen and Level 1 group against R12565 with a 12-inch screen and Level 2 group. These are examples from original VIN-matched labels; the stock numbers are not a promise of current availability.

What should you compare beyond the screen?

Separate the center touchscreen, driver display and any passenger display. Check the camera system by its exact name; a backup camera does not establish surround view. Laramie’s factory comparison includes ventilated outboard front seats, but use the sticker and a demonstration to check a particular truck. Heated seats are a different feature.

Does a higher trim include bed power and every camera?

No. The table identifies surround-view and bed-power entries as optional, with their source conditions. Confirm the equipment group, outlet location and installed power rating. A small cabin outlet does not establish an outlet in the bed, and the screen size does not establish which camera system is installed.

How should you decide whether the upgrade is worth it?

List the three features you will use most. Compare two actual VINs with similar cab, bed, engine and drive configuration, then compare their asking prices and equipment. Consider the value of the additional features to you; a trim badge alone cannot establish the better purchase. For towing, use the exact truck’s ratings and payload label.

What should you ask Sam or Ryan to show on video?

Ask for the radio information screen, camera views, front-seat ventilation controls and any bed outlet you need. Include the stock number or VIN. A short demonstration of your must-haves is more useful than assuming a package name means the same equipment in every model year.
